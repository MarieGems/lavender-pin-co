## The 5 Mistakes (original content is already excellent — specific, uses real Pinterest terminology, each mistake has a concrete FIX. Kept nearly verbatim, only tightened a few transitions.)

**01 — Your profile doesn't actually say what you sell**
Pinterest is a search engine before it's a social platform. If your bio is vague or brand-voice-only, Pinterest doesn't know what to rank you for — and neither does a stranger scrolling past.
**Fix:** Your bio should say, in plain words, who you help and what you offer. Save the personality for your pin captions.

**02 — Your board titles are cute, not searchable**
"Sparkle & Shine" sounds nice. It ranks for nothing. Boards are one of the biggest SEO levers on Pinterest, and a clever name with no keyword in it is a missed opportunity every single time someone searches.
**Fix:** Rename boards using the actual terms your buyer types into the search bar, not the term you'd use in a caption.

**03 — Your pin titles and descriptions don't match how people search**
There's a difference between how you talk about your product and how someone searches for it. If your pin title leads with personality instead of what the product actually is, Pinterest has nothing to match it to.
**Fix:** Lead with the keyword, then the benefit. Save the personality-driven language for the second half of the description.

**04 — You're pinning without checking what's already working**
Posting consistently feels productive, but if you're not looking at which pins are actually driving saves and clicks, you're guessing every time you create something new — and repeating whatever isn't working.
**Fix:** Check your top 5 performing pins once a month. Whatever they have in common — topic, format, wording — do more of that.

**05 — Your website link doesn't match the promise of the pin**
Someone clicks a pin because it promised them something specific. If the landing page doesn't deliver that exact thing fast, they bounce — and Pinterest notices when your links underperform.
**Fix:** Every pin should link to the page that matches its exact promise. Not your homepage. Not a collection page. The specific page.
